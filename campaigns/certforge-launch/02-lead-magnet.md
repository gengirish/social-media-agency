### THE COHORT CERTIFICATE CHECKLIST

*A 1-page checklist for anyone about to issue certificates to a group — a cohort, an internship batch, or an event.*

#### Before you issue

- [ ] **Name-spelling source of truth is locked.** Pick one list (LMS export, registration form, or sign-in sheet) as the only source you'll sign names from. Don't merge two lists at the last minute.
- [ ] **Recipients have confirmed their name as it should appear**, ideally in the format they'll use on LinkedIn or a resume — not just how they typed it at sign-up.
- [ ] **Someone owns the template.** One person (or role) is the single owner of the certificate design file, so nobody edits an old copy by mistake.
- [ ] **You've decided what a verifier needs.** A recruiter or employer should be able to confirm a certificate is real from the certificate itself (an ID or link) — not by emailing you and waiting.
- [ ] **You've written down your revocation policy** *before* issuing — what happens if someone drops out, is found to have cheated, or asks for a correction after the fact. Decide this now, not mid-crisis.
- [ ] **You've minimised the data you collect.** Only capture what the certificate needs to show (name, course/event, date). Skip fields like date of birth, address, or ID numbers unless a certificate genuinely requires them. A design that doesn't require the recipient to create an account is a good default — fewer places for their data to sit.

#### While you issue

- [ ] **Each certificate carries a QR code** (or short link) on the printable itself, not just in the email — so it's still checkable after it's downloaded, printed, or screenshotted.
- [ ] **You validate before you sign**, row by row — catch a misspelled name or wrong date *before* 60 certificates go out, not after.
- [ ] **You spot-check a handful of finished certificates** (not just the template) before sending the full batch — names, dates, and the verification link all resolve correctly.

#### After you issue

- [ ] **Certificates export to a portable, open format** — so recipients (and you) aren't locked into one vendor's viewer or app forever.
- [ ] **You have a written reissue policy**: what triggers a reissue (name correction, lost link, role/title change), and whether the old certificate is revoked when a new one is issued.
- [ ] **You know who to contact for a correction**, and how long it takes — recipients will ask.
- [ ] **You've kept a record of what you issued and to whom**, separate from the certificates themselves, in case you need to answer a verification question six months from now.
